Ship plan

How I'd ship Cortex

The autonomy dial per segment, where it sits on the trust ladder, how it deploys, what it returns, and how I keep it safe.

01 · Dial

Autonomy per segment

- Project PM → Supervised, “PM daily approver.” Every draft and queued story waits for the PM (built today).
- Engineering lead → Bounded-autonomous (desired, not built), “eng lead needs speed.” Low-risk risk flags skip the PM wait.
- Exec stakeholder → Assisted, “execs see approved only.”
The dial never moves the agent line: posting, dates and escalation choices stay human for everyone.
02 · Ladder

Rung + eval gate

Current rung: Supervised (every draft and queued story stops at the HITL checkpoint).
Eval gate to Bounded-autonomous (eng lead risk flags only): over 4 weeks and ≥ 20 runs, EV-5 jailbreak 100% (one failure resets the window), EV-1 and EV-4 ≥ 95%, 0 confidential leaks, ≥ 90% of drafts approved with no fact corrections. EV-1 fails today; routing must ship first.
Incident record: 0 production incidents (not deployed); 5 offline findings logged, including EV-5 failing before the injection guard.
03 · Deploy

Where it runs & who owns it

- Runtime: serverless functions, “fits short bounded runs.” Hook (primary) + cron (backup); each run capped at 6 steps, 60 s, $0.10.
- On-call owner: Matt Clark (portfolio PM, Cortex owner); engineering on-call backs up runtime failures.
- Rollback: CORTEX_OFF kill switch stops all runs; redeploy the last version that passed replay set R1–R5.
- Monitoring: runs/day and spend vs $1/day; exit types with an escalation-spike alert; injection-guard hits; critic rejections; PM approval rate.
04 · ROI

Value, not activity

- Outcome: ≥ 85% of triggered runs reach the HITL checkpoint with a PM-approved draft.
- Cost-to-serve: ~1 hour per update by hand → ≤ 15 min of review (target, not yet measured); ≤ $0.03 per run (observed 0.07–2.2¢).
- Trust incidents: 0 unapproved posts, 0 leaks, 0 invented dates.
Widen rule: up one notch when the gate is met and the operator signs off, one segment at a time; down one rung on the first incident, “trust is earned.”
05 · Governance

How I keep it safe

- Compliance: no secrets or personal data beyond names/handles in prompts; embargoed roadmap items stay out (filter planned, critic check 4 as backup).
- Safety: no post tool; posting, dates and escalation stay above the line for everyone; single-use post pass (planned); kill switch; injection guard.
- Reliability: caps of 6 steps, 60 s, $0.10/run, $1/day, 5 stories; escalate-on-stuck; model down → fail closed, PM writes it by hand.
- Strategy: widen into eng lead risk flags next, gated by EV-1, EV-4, EV-5, “keeps one consistent path.”
Build insights

What I learned

The honest three: where it hurt, what clicked, and the moment something changed.

😵 Friction

The critic: over-strict on commitments, yet blind to stories that refine work already shipped.

🧠 Learning

Bounds belong in code, not prompts: Cortex can’t post because there is no post tool.

💡 Aha

“Looking approved isn’t the same as being safe.” Unguarded, the jailbreak run ended as a critic-approved draft.
